How it works

Everything a QR symbol needs to be located is fixed by ISO/IEC 18004: three finder patterns, their separators, the timing rows, the alignment patterns, and two copies of the format information. Cover one corner and all of that is still recoverable from the other two. The bench rebuilds those modules, then treats each painted‑over data module as an erasure of known position.

That distinction is the whole trick. A block carrying n error‑correction codewords repairs n/2 codewords when it must find them first, but a full n when it is told where they are. The budget column shows 2 × errors + erasures against that limit; stay inside it and the decode is unique, so no other message could have produced the symbol.

The bench also re‑encodes its own answer and compares it against every module it could actually see. A conflict there means the grid or the payload is wrong, and the verdict drops to probable rather than quietly reporting a clean result.
